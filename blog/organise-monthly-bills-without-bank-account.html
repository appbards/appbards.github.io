    How to organise monthly bills without connecting your bank account

    By AppBards · 9 October 2026

    Rent is paid. The electricity bill has arrived, but you haven't paid it yet. A subscription renews next week. Keeping all of that in your head gets harder when bills arrive on different days.

    You can organise your monthly bills with a manual checklist. Write down what is due, add the amount when you know it, and mark each bill paid after you pay it. You can do this on paper, in a spreadsheet, or in a bill tracker that doesn't require a bank connection.

    1. Put your regular bills in one place

    Start with the bills you expect most months: rent or mortgage, electricity, water, internet, phone service, insurance and subscriptions. Add a separate entry for each bill, even if several come from the same provider.

    For each entry, record:

    • The bill's name
    • The amount, when known
    • The due date or usual due day
    • Whether it has been paid
    • A note if there is anything you need to check

    The useful question is: “What do I still need to pay this month?” A clear list helps you answer it without opening every email or account.

    2. Leave changing amounts blank until the bill arrives

    Some bills stay the same. Others change every month. Add an electricity bill to your list even if you haven't received the final amount yet.

    Leave its amount blank and update it when the bill arrives. An unknown amount is different from a zero-cost bill. If your checklist shows a remaining total, check how it handles blank amounts: the total may not include those bills yet.

    BillAmountPayment status
    RentConfirmedPaid
    ElectricityWaiting for the billUnpaid
    InternetConfirmedUnpaid

    That way, the electricity bill stays visible while you wait for its cost.

    3. Separate recurring bills from one-off expenses

    Recurring bills belong on next month's list too. One-off expenses, such as a repair, usually don't.

    If you use a spreadsheet, copy your recurring entries into the next month and reset their payment status. If you use an app, check how its recurring bills work. Last month's payment status should not make a new bill look paid.

    Review carried-over amounts as well. A previous amount can be a useful starting point, but a changed price or variable utility bill still needs updating.

    4. Mark bills paid after checking the payment

    Once you pay a bill, mark it paid on your checklist. For automatic payments, check that the payment went through before checking it off.

    A manual tracker records what you enter. It won't confirm a bank transaction for you.

    Keep paid entries visible or move them below unpaid ones. Either approach lets you see what you've already dealt with and what still needs attention.

    5. Add reminders where they help

    For bills you pay manually, set a reminder on the due date or before it. Choose a lead time that gives you enough time to act.

    Make sure notifications are enabled for the app you use. When you change a bill's due day, review its reminder too.

    6. Review the list at the start of each month

    Check for cancelled subscriptions, changed amounts and bills that no longer apply. Add any new regular expenses and review anything left unpaid from the previous month.

    A monthly bill checklist is useful for keeping track of payments. If you also want to track income, savings or every purchase, you may want a separate budget alongside it.

    Common questions

    Do I need to connect my bank to track bills?

    No. A manual checklist works with the information you enter. You update amounts and payment status yourself.

    Can a bill tracker pay bills for me?

    Monthly Bills tracks your checklist; it doesn't send payments or read your bank transactions. Pay through your usual provider or bank, then update the checklist.

    What if I don't know a bill's amount yet?

    Keep the bill on your list and enter its amount when it arrives. In Monthly Bills, blank amounts are excluded from totals until you add a cost.
