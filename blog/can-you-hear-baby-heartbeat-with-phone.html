    Can You Hear Your Baby's Heartbeat With a Phone?

    By App Bards · 12.11.2026 · Pregnancy & Audio Recording

    Pregnancy is full of moments you want to remember. From the first ultrasound to the first little kicks, it's natural to want to capture as much of the experience as possible.

    And since we use our phones for almost everything, you might have wondered: Can I hear my baby's heartbeat using just my smartphone?

    You've probably seen apps that promise to help you listen to pregnancy sounds using your phone's microphone. But what can these apps actually do, and how do you know what you're hearing?

    Here's what you should know before trying one.

    Can a Smartphone Really Hear a Baby's Heartbeat?

    A regular smartphone microphone cannot reliably detect or identify a baby's heartbeat during pregnancy.

    Your phone is designed to record everyday sounds, such as conversations, music, and videos. Its microphone may also capture quiet body sounds when placed close to your skin.

    However, hearing a rhythmic sound doesn't necessarily mean you're hearing your baby.

    Your own heartbeat, blood flow, digestion, movement, and even background noise can create sounds that resemble a heartbeat.

    This distinction matters because a phone recording cannot tell you whether your baby is healthy, and it should never be used for reassurance about your pregnancy.

    That doesn't mean your phone can't be useful for recording personal memories. It simply means understanding the difference between recording audio and medically detecting a fetal heartbeat.

    Why Can Different Sounds Resemble a Heartbeat?

    One of the most confusing things about pregnancy sound recording is that a rhythmic thumping sound can come from several sources.

    1. Your own heartbeat

    Your pulse creates a regular rhythm, and sounds associated with blood flow may be picked up when a microphone is positioned against your body.

    This can make a recording sound surprisingly similar to what someone expects a heartbeat to sound like.

    2. Digestion and other body sounds

    Your body naturally produces sounds as your digestive system works.

    Some of these can be low-pitched, repetitive, or difficult to identify from an audio recording alone.

    3. Phone movement

    Even very small movements can produce noticeable recording noise.

    Your fingers moving against the phone, clothing rubbing against the microphone, or a slight change in position can create tapping or thumping sounds.

    4. Background noise

    Air conditioning, fans, traffic, and household appliances can all appear in recordings.

    Sometimes you won't notice these sounds until you listen through headphones or amplify the recording.

    5. Audio enhancement

    Audio processing can make quieter sounds more noticeable.

    However, it may amplify unwanted noise as well.

    A louder or clearer recording does not mean the sound has been identified as a fetal heartbeat.

    This is why it's important to approach any recording as audio rather than a medical result.

    What's the Difference Between a Phone App and a Fetal Doppler?

    Although both may be associated with listening during pregnancy, they use very different technologies.

    A fetal Doppler is a specialized device that uses ultrasound to detect movement associated with blood flow.

    Healthcare professionals may use appropriate equipment as part of clinical assessment.

    A regular smartphone microphone, on the other hand, records acoustic sound.

    It cannot perform an ultrasound examination.

    Even home fetal Doppler devices can be misunderstood when used without appropriate training. A person may mistake their own pulse or other sounds for a baby's heartbeat.

    The NHS advises against using home Dopplers to check a baby's health. The FDA also warns about the risks of using fetal heartbeat monitoring equipment without appropriate medical supervision.

    If you want to hear your baby's heartbeat or have concerns about your pregnancy, your healthcare professional can explain which assessments are appropriate.

    How to Record Pregnancy Sounds With Your Android Phone

    If you'd like to preserve personal audio memories during pregnancy, you can make recordings using your Android phone.

    The process doesn't need to be complicated.

    Step 1: Find a quiet environment

    Choose a comfortable room with as little background noise as possible.

    Turn off nearby music or television if practical.

    The quieter the environment, the easier it is to listen to your recording afterward.

    Step 2: Check your phone's microphone

    Make sure your microphone isn't covered by your phone case, fingers, or clothing.

    Microphone placement varies between Android devices, so check where yours is located.

    Step 3: Start recording

    Open your preferred recording app.

    If you're recording near your abdomen, hold the phone comfortably without applying pressure.

    Try to keep your phone still to reduce handling noise.

    Step 4: Listen to your recording

    After recording, play back the audio.

    Headphones may make quieter recorded sounds easier to notice, although they cannot establish where those sounds came from.

    Step 5: Try audio enhancement

    Some recording apps offer controls for adjusting the captured sound.

    Hear My Baby Heartbeat Monitor includes audio enhancement controls that allow you to change how a recording sounds.

    Remember that enhancing a recording may also make background noise more noticeable.

    Step 6: Save your memory

    If you've captured a recording you'd like to keep, save it with a recognizable name.

    You might organize recordings by date or pregnancy milestone.

    You can also share personal recordings with family and friends.

    Important: These steps are intended for creating audio keepsakes, not locating a fetal heartbeat or checking fetal wellbeing.

    What if You Can't Hear Anything?

    It's possible to make a recording and hear very little, or nothing recognizable at all.

    That doesn't mean you've done something wrong, and it tells you nothing reliable about your baby's condition.

    Likewise, hearing a rhythmic sound doesn't confirm that your baby's heartbeat has been captured.

    Never use a smartphone recording to decide whether your pregnancy is progressing normally.

    If you're worried about your baby's movements or notice a change in their usual pattern, contact your midwife or maternity care team immediately. Don't wait to make another recording or use an app to check.

    A medical assessment is the appropriate way to address concerns about your baby's health.
